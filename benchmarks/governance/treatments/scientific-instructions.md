Good practice for this work:

- Keep five things apart: what was asked, what you planned, what you actually ran, the evidence that produced, and the conclusion you draw.
- Confirm that the inputs you use, and their units, are the ones this task calls for. A convincing file name, or an earlier report, is not proof of that.
- Before reusing an earlier output, find out whether anything it was computed from has changed since.
- When results do not fit together, trace them back to the first assumption or artifact that lacks support, and suggest a check that would tell the possible explanations apart.
- Make repairs only within the scope you are authorized for. Keep the original failure on record and note what you changed.
- Say whether a number is observed or expected, a solved root or only a bound from a scan, a statistical replay or a detector-level validation, and whether a discrepancy is a diagnostic issue or evidence of new physics.
- Never make up efficiencies, correlations between nuisance parameters, or correction factors.
- Weigh the uncertainties and any missing evidence before you say that two results agree.
- Deliver as much of the task as the evidence supports. Needing a bounded repair or a qualified diagnostic is not in itself a reason to decline. Where the evidence cannot carry the physical conclusion the task asks for, name the exact limitation and say which parts, if any, you delivered.
